Prototype: Automatic Battle

A two-player, automatic combat prototype built in under a week.

What it is

Slavic Battle is a two-player, automatic combat prototype built in under a week. Each round, both players spend gold to place units: Soldier, Tank, Ranger and Mage, across three lanes, then watch them clash automatically; whoever wins 2 of 3 lanes takes the round, and whoever wins 2 rounds wins the match. Designed with rock-paper-scissors-style unit counters in mind, the whole game hinges on getting the four units' stats to feel fair and distinct from each other.

What I did / experience gained

I originated the core game concept, then led QA and balance for the project: running extensive playtesting to make sure all four units felt fair and distinct against each other, rather than any one composition dominating. This meant testing in isolated match-ups first (soldiers vs. mages, tanks vs. rangers, and so on) to tune individual stats, then moving to mirror battles to rule out positional advantages, and finally full playtests across the team and beyond to catch what isolated testing couldn't.

Owning balance on a game built in under a week taught me how to prioritize under a tight timeline: deciding which testing method would catch the most meaningful problems fastest, and trusting a structured process (isolated → mirrored → open playtests) instead of guessing at numbers. It also sharpened my eye for spotting when a “feels off” reaction from a playtester points to a real balance issue versus an unlucky match.
